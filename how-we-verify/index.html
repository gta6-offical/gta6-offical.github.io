How we verify GTA 6 facts

Every claim on this site carries an implied label: confirmed, reported or unknown. This page explains the method so you can judge for yourself.

Updated November 14, 2026

Contents

    GTA 6 attracts rumours, fake leaks and recycled headlines. We use a simple, repeatable method to keep facts apart from guesses. You can apply the same method to any page about the game, including ours.

    Labels and source order

    Evidence labels used on this site
    LabelMeaningExample
    ConfirmedStated on an official Rockstar page or in a Take-Two filing that we read directlyRelease date of 19 November 2026
    ReportedPublished by a news outlet; not checked against an official page; written with attributionUS$79.99 and US$99.99 price points
    UnknownNot announced, or seen only in snippets; we do not state it as factPC version, PEGI rating
    BackgroundLong-established public knowledge outside GTA VI news, such as series history or real geographyGTA V released in 2013; the Everglades are in south Florida

    Source order

    1. Rockstar's official page, rockstargames.com/VI, for the game's name, date, platforms, editions, setting and rating label.
    2. Take-Two filings and earnings releases on the SEC site for business statements, such as the 9 November 2026 reaffirmation of the date.
    3. Established news outlets for details the first two do not cover, always with attribution.
    4. Everything else is treated as unverified and kept out of the text.

    Background is labelled too

    Series history, such as the years of earlier games on the history page, is public knowledge and not part of the GTA VI fact sheet. The same goes for real places in Florida: when Leonida's Florida roots describes the Everglades or Miami Beach, it describes the real world, not what appears in the game. We say this on those pages.

    How the method works in practice

    What we do not publish

    • Leaks, stolen footage or datamined content.
    • Map sizes, system requirements, file sizes, review scores or sales numbers that nobody official has released.
    • PC dates, PS5 Pro details or PEGI numbers without a primary source.
    • Piracy, cracks, "free download" links, cheats for online play or gambling content.

    The list of open items is kept on what is still unknown.

    A worked example: the price

    Several outlets report US$79.99 for Standard and US$99.99 for Ultimate. We have not seen the figures on an official store page. So we write "reportedly" and tell readers to check the store, as on editions explained. Compare the release date: it is on Rockstar's page and in Take-Two's 8-K, so we write it as a plain fact. If an official store later shows a different price for your country, the store wins, and we update the page rather than defend the earlier report.

    A second example: the unlock time

    Some sites claim a specific global hour. Rockstar has not published one, and the reports we saw on storefront times were too thin to rely on. So we say only that no single global time has been announced and point readers to their own store listing, as on the pre-order and preload guide.

    Why summaries need care

    Some pages we use are long or only partly available to us, and automated summaries can garble details. When our notes flag a risk, such as the middle step of the delay history, we attribute the claim and avoid stating more than the sources support. That is why the delay history in why GTA 6 matters says "according to reports".

    Dates, limits and your own checks

    Dates and updates

    Every page is written as of 14 November 2026. We phrase things so they remain accurate after launch, for example "release date: 19 November 2026" rather than "coming in five days". When a fact changes, the page changes; the glossary and unknowns pages are updated first because they gather the most-searched questions.

    Limits of our method

    No method is perfect. Some details may change after we read them, and we cannot check store prices in every country. That is why many entries tell you to confirm on the store, and why reported items are always attributed. We would rather publish a short, honest answer than a long, impressive guess. Our team is independent; no company has paid for or reviewed our pages, and the site carries no adverts for downloads or key sellers.

    How to check a GTA 6 claim yourself

    1. Who said it, and is it Rockstar or Take-Two?
    2. Is there a date and a link to the primary page?
    3. Is it a fact, a forecast or an opinion?
    4. Do two independent outlets cite the same primary source, or just each other?
    5. Does it include a specific number nobody has released, such as a map size?
    6. Does it ask you to click, log in or pay? Then read how to avoid GTA 6 scams first.

    If you find an error on this site, tell the site owner. We correct mistakes and note meaningful changes. Who we are is on about this site, and the terms are defined in the glossary.

    Frequently asked questions

    Q1How do you decide what is confirmed?

    A fact is confirmed only if it is on Rockstar’s official page or in a Take-Two filing that we read directly.

    Q2What does 'reported' mean?

    It means a news outlet published it and we have not checked it against an official page, so we attribute it.

    Q3Do you publish GTA 6 leaks?

    No. We do not publish leaks, datamined content, or unverified numbers.

    Q4Why do you not state a PEGI rating?

    We have not verified it on an official PEGI source, so it stays listed as unknown.

    Q5Is the Florida information about the game?

    No. Real-world geography is background about what inspired the setting, labelled as such, not a description of game content.

    Q6Which source do you trust most?

    Rockstar’s official page, followed by Take-Two’s SEC filings.

    Q7How can I report a mistake?

    Contact the site owner. We correct errors and update the page.

    Q8Are you affiliated with Rockstar Games?

    No. We are an independent fan editorial team, not affiliated with Rockstar Games or Take-Two Interactive.
