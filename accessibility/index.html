Setup & Safety

Accessibility and comfort settings for GTA 6 players

Rockstar has not announced GTA VI's in-game accessibility options. Your PS5 or Xbox already has many tools, and this page shows where they are and what they do.

Updated November 14, 2026

Contents

    Players with low vision, hearing loss, limited mobility or motion sensitivity want to know one thing before buying: will I be able to play comfortably? For Grand Theft Auto VI, the honest answer starts with a gap. Rockstar has not published a list of in-game accessibility options as of 14 November 2026. What you can control today is the console, and both PS5 and Xbox Series X|S offer far more than many people realise.

    Not announced: subtitle styles, colour-blind modes, aim assistance levels, difficulty options and control remapping inside GTA VI have not been detailed by Rockstar. Lists claiming to describe the game's settings menu are guesses until the game or an official page shows them. Independent reviews and accessibility-focused writers usually cover this soon after release.

    Console tools you can switch on now

    These system-level settings apply across games and menus. Names below are the ones Sony and Microsoft use in their Settings apps; menus move around after system updates, so use the search or help function on your console if a label differs.

    Vision

    Screen Reader on PS5 and Narrator on Xbox read out system menus, store pages and notifications, which helps with buying, installing and updating a game. Whether they read GTA VI's own menus depends on Rockstar's implementation, which is not announced. Text size, high contrast and colour filters affect system screens; colour filters on Xbox and colour correction on PS5 can also tint the whole picture, including games, which some players with colour vision deficiency find useful.

    Hearing

    Mono audio sends both stereo channels to each ear, useful if you hear well on one side only. Visual cues matter a great deal in open-world games, where sirens and radio chatter carry information, so in-game subtitles will be the key setting once the game's menu is known. Earlier Rockstar games, including GTA V, offered subtitles for dialogue, so it is reasonable to look for them, but their exact options in GTA VI are not confirmed.

    Mobility and controllers

    Both consoles let you swap buttons at system level. Sony's Access controller, released in December 2023, is a configurable pad designed for players who find a standard controller hard to hold. Microsoft's Xbox Adaptive Controller, launched in 2018, accepts external switches and buttons, and Copilot lets a second person help with the same inputs. System remapping can clash with a game's own layout, so after release, check the in-game controls screen and test a few minutes before changing many buttons.

    Comfort, motion and long sessions

    If camera movement makes you queasy

    • Sit further from the screen and keep the room lit rather than playing in the dark.
    • Look in the game's settings, once available, for camera shake, motion blur, field of view or camera distance options. Many open-world games include some of these, but GTA VI's list is not announced.
    • Driving at speed in third person is a common trigger. Short sessions with breaks help most people adjust.
    • Stop at the first sign of nausea. Pushing through usually makes it worse.

    Breaks and play time

    Big story games invite very long sessions. Both consoles show play time and let you set schedules or reminders through family and account settings; adults can use the same tools on their own account. A break every hour is a simple habit for eyes, wrists and posture.

    Reading the screen from the sofa

    If on-screen text is hard to read, check the TV's picture mode before anything else. A "game" mode reduces input lag, and turning off overscan or zoom stops the edges of the picture, where maps and prompts often sit, from being cut off. HDR calibration screens on both consoles are worth a minute too.

    Before and after you buy

    A short checklist

    1. Set up system-level text, contrast and audio options now, before launch.
    2. Configure any adaptive controller and test remapping in a game you already own.
    3. After 19 November 2026, read the game's settings menu or an accessibility review before deciding how to play.
    4. If the console is shared, give each player a profile so personal settings do not get overwritten.

    Bottom line: the console gives you a solid base today. The game's own accessibility menu is the missing piece, and we will not describe it until it is official on Rockstar's GTA VI page or visible in the released game.

    Frequently asked questions

    Q1Does GTA 6 have accessibility options?

    Rockstar has not announced GTA VI’s in-game accessibility options as of 14 November 2026. Console-level settings on PS5 and Xbox are available regardless.

    Q2Does GTA 6 have subtitles?

    Not confirmed in detail. Earlier Rockstar games such as GTA V had dialogue subtitles, so they are likely to be looked for, but GTA VI’s options are not announced.

    Q3Is there a colour-blind mode in GTA 6?

    Not announced. Xbox colour filters and PS5 colour correction can adjust the whole picture at system level.

    Q4Can I remap buttons for GTA 6?

    Both PS5 and Xbox let you remap buttons system-wide. Whether GTA VI has its own remapping menu has not been announced.

    Q5Can I use the PS5 Access controller or Xbox Adaptive Controller?

    Both work at system level on their consoles. Rockstar has not published specific GTA VI support notes for them.

    Q6How can I reduce motion sickness in open-world games?

    Take short sessions, sit further back, keep the room lit and use camera shake or motion blur settings if the game offers them.

    Q7Will the console screen reader read GTA 6 menus?

    It reads system menus and stores. Whether it reads the game’s own menus depends on Rockstar, which has not said.
